# Tailgating and Physical Security

## 1. Lesson Goals

By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:

- define tailgating and physical social engineering
- explain why physical access affects cybersecurity
- identify risks around doors, badges, visitors, desks, and devices
- suggest safe physical security habits
- connect physical security to data protection
- write scenario-based defensive responses

### English Explanation

Cybersecurity does not only happen on a computer screen. Physical access in the real world can also affect system security.

Examples:

- unauthorized person enters a server room
- someone follows a student into a restricted area
- password is written on a monitor
- computer is left unlocked
- sensitive documents are left on a desk
- someone watches a password being typed

**Tailgating** means an unauthorized person follows an authorized person into a restricted area. Defensive action does not require students to confront strangers; they should follow school procedures and inform a teacher, reception, or security.

## 5. Real-life Example

### Example: Open Classroom Computer

A student leaves a classroom computer logged in. Another person could access files, send messages, or change work under that student's account.

Safe habit: lock the screen or log out before leaving.

---

## 6. Defensive Recognition Pattern

```text
Physical access request or unusual presence
→ do not bypass access control
→ avoid confrontation if unsafe
→ follow visitor/security procedure
→ inform responsible staff
→ secure devices and documents
```

---

## 7. Scenario Analysis

### Scenario

A person carrying boxes asks a student to hold the secure door open because they “forgot their badge”.

| Warning Sign | Explanation |
|---|---|
| no badge | cannot prove authorization |
| emotional pressure | person is carrying boxes |
| restricted access | secure door |
| bypass request | asks student to break normal process |

---

## 8. Explanation of the Scenario

This could be tailgating. The student should not provide unauthorized access. A safe response is to politely direct the person to reception or ask a staff member to verify.

---

## 9. Step-by-step Defensive Response

| Step | Defensive Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Do not hold secure doors for unknown people |
| 2 | Avoid confrontation if unsafe |
| 3 | Direct visitors to reception or staff |
| 4 | Report unusual access attempts |
| 5 | Lock devices when unattended |
| 6 | Keep sensitive papers and passwords out of view |
| 7 | Challenge through official process, not personal conflict |

---

## 10. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is a problem | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Holding doors to be polite | Can bypass access control | Direct visitor to reception |
| Leaving screens unlocked | Others can act under your account | Lock screen before leaving |
| Writing passwords on paper nearby | Anyone can view or copy them | Use password manager or secure method |
| Confronting aggressively | May create safety risk | Follow reporting procedure |
| Ignoring physical security in CS | Physical access can lead to data access | Connect physical and digital security |

## 13. Exam-style / Reflection Questions

### Question 1 [3 marks]

Explain why tailgating is a security risk.

<details><summary>Mark Scheme Style Answer</summary>

Tailgating allows an unauthorized person to enter a restricted area by following someone with access. This may give them physical access to devices, documents, or network equipment, which could lead to data theft or system compromise.

</details>

### Question 2 [4 marks]

A student leaves a computer logged in. Explain two risks.

<details><summary>Mark Scheme Style Answer</summary>

Another person could access or copy the student's files. They could also send messages or make changes using the student's account, making it appear as if the student performed those actions.

</details>
