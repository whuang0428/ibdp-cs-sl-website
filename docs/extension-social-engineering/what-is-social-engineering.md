# What is Social Engineering?

## 1. Lesson Goals

By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:

- define **social engineering**
- explain why attackers target people, not only technology
- distinguish technical attacks and manipulation-based attacks
- identify common social engineering channels
- explain ethical and defensive boundaries
- write exam-style explanations of social engineering risk

### 中文讲解

**Social engineering（社会工程学）** 是一种利用人的心理、信任、紧张、好奇、压力或习惯来获得信息或让人做出不安全行为的攻击方式。

### English Explanation

**Social engineering** is a type of attack that uses human psychology, trust, urgency, curiosity, pressure, or routine to obtain information or make people perform unsafe actions.

It is different from a purely technical attack. A technical attack may directly exploit a software vulnerability, while social engineering often targets the human part of the system. Examples include:

- tricking a user into clicking a fake link
- pretending to be a teacher, colleague, or IT support worker
- asking for passwords or verification codes
- persuading a user to open a malicious attachment
- convincing someone to bypass a normal approval process

The purpose of studying social engineering is not to attack people. The goal is to recognize risks and protect yourself, your school, and organizational data.

## 5. Real-life Example

### Example: Fake IT Support Message

A student receives a message:

> Your school account will be closed today. Send your password to IT support now.

This is suspicious because real IT staff should not ask for a password. The message also creates urgency to pressure the student.

---

## 6. Defensive Recognition Pattern

```text
Unexpected request
→ asks for sensitive information or unsafe action
→ creates pressure, fear, reward, or urgency
→ verify using trusted channel
→ do not act until verified
→ report if suspicious
```

---

## 7. Scenario Analysis

### Scenario

A message appears to come from a teacher. It asks students to open a link and log in again to “confirm exam registration”. The link does not use the normal school domain.

| Warning Sign | Explanation |
|---|---|
| unexpected login request | user did not expect this action |
| suspicious link | not normal school domain |
| urgency | exam registration pressure |
| credential request | asks for username/password |

---

## 8. Explanation of the Scenario

The request may be trying to capture login details. The safest response is not to click the link. Students should verify through a trusted channel, such as the official LMS or school email system, and report the suspicious message.

---

## 9. Step-by-step Defensive Response

| Step | Defensive Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pause and do not click immediately |
| 2 | Check sender address and domain carefully |
| 3 | Look for pressure, threats, or unusual requests |
| 4 | Verify through an official channel |
| 5 | Do not share passwords or verification codes |
| 6 | Report the message to the correct person/team |
| 7 | If already clicked, change password and report quickly |

---

## 10. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is a problem | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking cybersecurity is only technical | People are often targeted | Include human factors |
| Replying to verify | Reply may go to attacker | Use a trusted separate channel |
| Sharing passwords with 'IT' | Legitimate support should not need passwords | Never share passwords or codes |
| Ignoring urgency signs | Pressure is a common manipulation technique | Pause before acting |
| Treating training as attack practice | Unsafe and unethical | Keep activities defensive and fictional |

## 11. Guided Practice

### Practice 1

Why might an attacker target a person instead of a computer system?

<details><summary>Suggested Answer</summary>

A person may be easier to manipulate than a secured computer system. If the attacker tricks a user into revealing a password or approving access, they may bypass technical protections.

</details>

### Practice 2

What should you do if a message asks for your password?

<details><summary>Suggested Answer</summary>

Do not share the password. Verify the request through an official channel and report the message if suspicious.

</details>

## 13. Exam-style / Reflection Questions

### Question 1 [2 marks]

State what is meant by social engineering.

<details><summary>Mark Scheme Style Answer</summary>

Social engineering is manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security.

</details>

### Question 2 [4 marks]

A student receives a message asking for their school password. Explain why this is suspicious and what they should do.

<details><summary>Mark Scheme Style Answer</summary>

The message is suspicious because legitimate support staff should not ask for a user's password. The student should not reply or click links. They should verify the request using an official channel and report the message to the school or IT support.

</details>
