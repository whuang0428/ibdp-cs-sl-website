# OSINT and Digital Footprints

## 1. Lesson Goals

By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:

- define OSINT and digital footprint
- explain how public information can increase risk
- identify examples of information that should not be overshared
- explain privacy settings and data minimization
- connect public information to spear phishing risk
- create safe digital footprint habits

### English Explanation

**OSINT** means information collected from publicly available sources. It is not automatically illegal or malicious, but if a person shares too much public information, attackers may use it for spear phishing or impersonation.

A **digital footprint** is the data left behind by online activity, such as:

- social media profiles
- school, course, or event information
- location or uniforms in photos
- public email addresses
- username patterns
- public comments and posts

The goal is not to stop students from using the internet. The goal is to control public information and reduce unnecessary exposure.

## 5. Real-life Example

### Example: Spear Phishing from Public Details

A student posts:

> Excited for tomorrow's IBDP CS database test with Mr Lee!

The next day, they receive a fake message:

> Mr Lee shared the database test review file. Log in here.

The attacker used public details to make the message more believable.

---

## 6. Defensive Recognition Pattern

```text
Before posting:
What information is visible?
Who can see it?
Could it identify school, schedule, teacher, location, or account?
Is it necessary to share publicly?
Could privacy settings reduce exposure?
```

---

## 7. Scenario Analysis

### Scenario

A student uses the same username on gaming platforms, school forums, and social media. Their social media profile also shows school name and class schedule.

| Risk | Explanation |
|---|---|
| username linking | accounts can be connected |
| school identification | attacker can customize messages |
| schedule exposure | attacker knows timing |
| spear phishing | messages become more believable |

---

## 8. Explanation of the Scenario

The student has increased their digital footprint. Attackers could combine public information to create targeted messages. Defensive steps include reviewing privacy settings, limiting public schedule information, and using different account identities where appropriate.

---

## 9. Step-by-step Defensive Response

| Step | Defensive Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Review what is publicly visible |
| 2 | Remove unnecessary personal or schedule information |
| 3 | Adjust privacy settings |
| 4 | Avoid sharing passwords, codes, IDs, or sensitive documents |
| 5 | Be careful with repeated usernames |
| 6 | Think before posting location or event details |
| 7 | Report impersonation or suspicious targeted messages |

---

## 10. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is a problem | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking public posts cannot be risky | Public details can support targeted attacks | Limit unnecessary exposure |
| Using same username everywhere | Accounts can be linked | Separate identities where appropriate |
| Sharing schedules publicly | Timing can be exploited | Use private channels |
| Assuming privacy settings solve everything | Screenshots and resharing still exist | Share carefully |
| Blaming students for being online | Goal is safer participation | Teach control and awareness |

## 11. Guided Practice

### Practice 1

How can public information help spear phishing?

<details><summary>Suggested Answer</summary>

It can help an attacker customize a message using real names, events, courses, or schedules, making the message more believable.

</details>

### Practice 2

Give one example of data minimization.

<details><summary>Suggested Answer</summary>

Only sharing the information needed for a purpose, such as not posting a full class schedule publicly.

</details>

## 13. Exam-style / Reflection Questions

### Question 1 [2 marks]

State what is meant by digital footprint.

<details><summary>Mark Scheme Style Answer</summary>

A digital footprint is the data or information left behind by a person's online activity.

</details>

### Question 2 [4 marks]

Explain how OSINT can increase the risk of spear phishing.

<details><summary>Mark Scheme Style Answer</summary>

Publicly available information can reveal names, roles, school events, schedules, or interests. An attacker can use this information to create a message that appears relevant and trustworthy to the target, increasing the chance that the target clicks a link or shares information.

</details>
