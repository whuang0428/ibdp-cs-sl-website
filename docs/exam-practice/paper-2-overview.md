# Paper 2 Overview

## Page Purpose

Paper 2 is the **option-based / applied paper**. It is different from Paper 1 because students prepare one selected option area and practise applying that knowledge to exam-style scenarios.

Use this page to understand:

```text
what Paper 2 is for
how Paper 2 is different from Paper 1
why your class option matters
how to prepare without trying to revise every possible option
which existing website pages can support your preparation
```

This page is an overview and learning route. It is not a full Paper 2 question bank yet.

---

## Paper 1 and Paper 2

| Paper | Main focus | What students practise |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Core syllabus understanding and general problem-solving | Theme A, Theme B, command terms, algorithms, SQL, short and extended scenario answers |
| Paper 2 | One selected option area and applied exam-style responses | Option-specific concepts, scenario interpretation, longer explanations, and mark scheme language |

Paper 1 asks students to show broad understanding across the core course. Paper 2 asks students to apply knowledge from the option their class is studying.

::: warning Check Your Option
SL students need to know which Paper 2 option their class is preparing. Do not try to prepare all options equally unless your teacher has asked you to compare them for revision.
:::

## How to Prepare

1. Know which option your class is studying.
2. Learn the core concepts of that option.
3. Practise option-specific command terms.
4. Compare answers with mark scheme language.
5. Review common scenario-based questions.

---

## Paper 2 Study Route

### 1. Identify the option

Write down the exact option your class is preparing. Your revision should match that option.

```text
My Paper 2 option: ______________________________
```

### 2. Build a concept checklist

For your option, make a short checklist of the most important ideas. For example:

```text
key vocabulary
main processes
diagrams or models
advantages and disadvantages
ethical, social, or practical issues
common scenario patterns
```

### 3. Practise command terms

Paper 2 answers still depend on command terms. Use [Command Terms](./command-terms) to check what each question is asking you to do.

Examples:

| Command term | Paper 2 answer habit |
|---|---|
| State / identify | Give a direct option-specific term or fact. |
| Outline | Give a point with a small amount of detail. |
| Explain | Give a point and show how or why it matters in the scenario. |
| Compare | Use clear similarities and differences. |
| Evaluate / discuss | Give balanced points, limitations, and a supported judgement. |

### 4. Use scenario detail

Paper 2 often rewards applied answers. Do not only write general textbook definitions.

Weak answer:

```text
Normalization is useful because it is organized.
```

Stronger answer:

```text
Normalization reduces repeated customer data across tables, which can reduce update errors when the business changes a customer's address.
```

The stronger answer uses:

```text
technical concept + explanation + scenario impact
```

### 5. Compare with mark scheme language

After each practice answer, use [Mark Scheme Skills](./mark-scheme-skills) to check whether your answer has:

- correct technical vocabulary
- clear separate points
- enough expansion for the marks
- links to the scenario
- no vague phrases such as "better", "good", or "easy" without explanation

---

## Option Preparation Checklist

Before a Paper 2 practice paper, check that you can:

- [ ] name your selected option
- [ ] explain the main concepts in that option
- [ ] use option-specific vocabulary accurately
- [ ] answer short command-term questions
- [ ] write developed explanations linked to scenarios
- [ ] compare advantages and disadvantages
- [ ] discuss limitations, risks, or trade-offs
- [ ] use mark scheme language when improving answers
- [ ] avoid revising unrelated options at the expense of your selected option
